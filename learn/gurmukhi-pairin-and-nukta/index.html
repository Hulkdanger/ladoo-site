Pairin and nukta: the last nine shapes of Gurmukhi

By the Ladoo team · Published August 31, 2026

After the 35 core letters and the vowel signs, nine shapes remain: three subjoined pairin letters that tuck under a consonant to blend it, and six nukta letters, ordinary letters wearing a dot that shifts their sound. They are the last stop before you can read anything Punjabi throws at you, including the first line of the Mool Mantar.

The three pairin letters

Pairin means "at the feet": a small rara, haha, or vava written under a consonant blends into it. ਪ੍ਰ reads "pra". The buried haha is the subtle one, since it often surfaces as tone rather than an audible h, which is why ਪੜ੍ਹ (read) does not sound like "parh" spoken by an English reader.

੍ਰ ਪੈਰੀਂ ਰਾਰਾ (pairin rara) r (blended under a letter)

A small copy of Rara tucked under another letter. The two sounds blend into one quick cluster, so ਪ + ੍ਰ reads 'pr'.

੍ਹ ਪੈਰੀਂ ਹਾਹਾ (pairin haha) h (breath under a letter, often tone)

A small Haha hiding under a letter. It adds a light breath that in everyday speech usually turns into a tone, as in ਪੜ੍ਹ.

੍ਵ ਪੈਰੀਂ ਵਾਵਾ (pairin vava) v/w (blended under a letter)

A rare little Vava under a letter, adding a quick w glide. You will mostly meet it in careful or classical spellings like ਸ੍ਵਰ.

The six nukta letters

The dot (nukta) under these letters marks sounds that entered Punjabi through Persian, Arabic, and English loanwords: z, f, x, and friends. In relaxed speech many speakers fold them back into the base letter, saying "jaroor" for ਜ਼ਰੂਰ, so treat the dotted sounds as the careful form and you will understand both.

ਸ਼ ਸ਼ੱਸ਼ਾ (shha) ʃ

Sassa (ਸ) with a dot below becomes 'sh'.

ਖ਼ ਖ਼ੱਖ਼ਾ (khha) x

Khakha (ਖ) with a dot below: a raspy 'kh' from the throat.

ਗ਼ ਗ਼ੱਗ਼ਾ (ghha) ɣ

Gagga (ਗ) with a dot below: a soft throaty 'gh'.

ਜ਼ ਜ਼ੱਜ਼ਾ (za) z

Jajja (ਜ) with a dot below becomes 'z'.

ਫ਼ ਫ਼ੱਫ਼ਾ (fa) f

Phappha (ਫ) with a dot below becomes a true 'f'.

ਲ਼ ਲ਼ੱਲ਼ਾ (lla) ɭ

Lalla (ਲ) with a dot below: a retroflex 'l'. Regional; recognition only.

Do you need them on day one?

No. The core 35 letters and vowel signs carry you through most beginner text, and that is the order the app teaches. But names, song lyrics, and newspapers use pairin and nukta constantly, so they are worth a focused week once the core letters feel solid.
