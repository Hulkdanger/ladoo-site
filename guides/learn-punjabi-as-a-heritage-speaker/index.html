How to learn Punjabi as a heritage speaker

By the Ladoo team · Published August 24, 2026

You follow every word at the dinner table. Your aunts talk over each other and you keep up fine. Then someone asks you a question in Punjabi and your answer comes out in English. This has a name, receptive bilingualism, it is one of the most common language situations in the diaspora, and it is reversible faster than you think.

Why you understand but can't speak

Understanding and speaking are different skills stored in different ways. Growing up, you got thousands of hours of listening practice for free, and almost zero hours of being required to produce Punjabi, because the adults switched to English the moment you hesitated. Comprehension grew; production never got trained. Nothing is wrong with you and nothing was lost. The speaking circuit was just never built. We wrote more about how this happens in why diaspora kids lose Punjabi.

Your head start is real

A heritage speaker starting to speak is not a beginner. You already hear the difference between ਤ and ਟ, which takes new learners months. You have hundreds of words stored passively, kinship terms, food, commands, the emotional vocabulary of home. Your accent foundations are already native-shaped. Most beginners need two to three months to hold a simple conversation; heritage speakers who practice speaking daily usually get there in weeks, because they are converting vocabulary they already own rather than learning it from zero.

The plan

Start speaking on day one, out loud, even alone. The freeze is a production problem, so only production fixes it: reading silently and tapping multiple-choice answers will not. Say full sentences, record yourself, and get feedback on what landed. This is the reason every Ladoo lesson includes a speak-out-loud exercise with word-by-word feedback, and why Bhaji, the AI tutor, exists: it gives you a patient conversation partner who never switches to English out of pity.

Learn Gurmukhi in parallel, not later. The script is phonetic, 35 letters, and most learners read simple words within two to three weeks of short daily practice. Reading unlocks song lyrics, texts from family, and the difference between what you think an aunt said and what she actually said. Start with the free alphabet chart with audio.

Then use the family you have. Tell one relative you are practicing and ask them to answer you in Punjabi even when you reply in English. Ten minutes of real conversation a week with a grandmother beats an hour of silent review, and it gives the practice a point, which is the thing that keeps you going.

The embarrassment problem

The biggest blocker for heritage speakers is not grammar, it is shame: speaking badly in front of people who expected you to know the language. Practice somewhere with no audience first. Build the sentences in private until they stop wobbling, then take them to the dinner table. The relatives who tease you will also be the proudest when you answer back.

Common questions

Is it too late to learn Punjabi as an adult?

No. Adults learn vocabulary and grammar faster than children; children only win on accent, and as a heritage speaker your accent foundations are already set from childhood listening. The window that matters is the one you open by starting.

Should I skip the beginner lessons?

Skim, don't skip. You will fly through early material, and that is useful: easy wins rebuild the habit of producing Punjabi out loud. In Ladoo the two tracks are separate, so you can start speaking practice at your real level while learning the script from zero.

Do I need Gurmukhi to speak Punjabi?

Strictly, no. But romanized Punjabi hides the sounds that matter, and the script takes weeks, not months. Learners who read Gurmukhi stop guessing at pronunciation, because the script writes exactly what is said.
