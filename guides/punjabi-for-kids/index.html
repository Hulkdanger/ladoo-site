Punjabi for kids: raising a Punjabi speaker in the diaspora

By the Ladoo team · Published August 31, 2026

The pattern repeats in almost every immigrant family: the first generation speaks Punjabi, the second understands it but answers in English, and the third barely recognizes it. Three generations is all it takes, and the middle generation, the one reading this page, is where the fade either continues or stops. The fixes are unglamorous and they work.

The one rule that matters most

Kids learn the language they need, not the language they hear. A child who hears Punjabi all day but is answered in English when she replies in English learns, correctly, that Punjabi is optional. The single highest-leverage habit is to keep responding in Punjabi even when your child answers in English, gently and without making it a fight. Understanding stays alive as long as the input continues, and speaking follows when the need appears. We wrote about the mechanics of the fade in why diaspora kids lose Punjabi.

What works at each age

Under 5: talk, sing, and narrate in Punjabi as much as you can stand. At this age quantity of natural speech beats every product and every class. Lullabies, kitchen commentary, and boliyan count more than flashcards.

Ages 5 to 10: this is when Gurmukhi can start. The script is phonetic and the letters are drawable, which is why finger tracing works so well at this age; hands remember shapes that eyes forget. Short app sessions land here too, ten minutes counts. Stories matter more than drills: a child who wants to know how the story ends is a child practicing Punjabi voluntarily.

Tweens and teens: motivation shifts from pleasing you to owning an identity. Music does heavy lifting here, Punjabi lyrics are a genuine reading curriculum, and so does texting relatives in Punjab. A teen who wants the language for their own reasons will outrun any curriculum you impose.

Gurdwara Punjabi schools

Weekend Punjabi classes at the gurdwara remain the backbone of diaspora Punjabi education, and they bring the two things no app has: a room full of other kids doing the same thing, and elders who talk to children in real Punjabi. Their common limit is practice time between classes, seven days is a long gap for a six-year-old's memory. Common questions

What age should a child start learning Gurmukhi?

Around five to seven, once English letters have stopped being confusing on their own. Earlier exposure does no harm, but formal letter practice lands best when a child can already hold a crayon with intent and sit for ten minutes.

Will Punjabi confuse my child or slow their English?

No. Decades of bilingualism research are unambiguous: children handle two languages without harm to either, and the diaspora's actual problem is the opposite one, English crowding Punjabi out. The risk you are managing is loss, not confusion.

I barely speak Punjabi myself. Can I still pass it on?

Yes, and thousands of second-generation parents are doing it right now. Learn alongside your child, out loud, imperfectly. A parent who is visibly learning gives the child permission to be imperfect too, which is worth more than a flawless accent. Start with our heritage speaker guide.
