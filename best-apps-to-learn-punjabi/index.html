The best apps to learn Punjabi in 2026

By the Ladoo team · Published August 31, 2026

First, the disclosure: we make Ladoo, one of the apps below. We wrote this page anyway because the roundups that rank for this search rarely mention what Punjabi learners actually care about, like whether an app teaches Gurmukhi at all. Every strength we list for a competitor is real. Where Ladoo is not the right pick, we say so.

The short version: Punjabi is badly served by the big platforms. Duolingo does not offer Punjabi, Babbel does not either, and Rosetta Stone dropped it years ago. The realistic options are a handful of dedicated apps and a tutor marketplace, and the right one depends on whether you want to speak, read Gurmukhi, or both.

1. Ladoo: best for speaking and Gurmukhi, best free tier

Ladoo is the most complete app for speaking Punjabi and reading Gurmukhi, built for the diaspora. It teaches only Punjabi. The Language track runs from greetings to full sentences with speaking exercises that give word-by-word pronunciation feedback, and a separate Gurmukhi track teaches all 35 letters, vowel signs, and numerals from zero with native audio and finger tracing. Bhaji, the AI tutor, holds real spoken conversations in Punjabi, remembers your mistakes, and drills them. Eight illustrated stories are narrated line by line.

Every lesson is free, with no lesson ever locked, which makes Ladoo's free tier the largest of the dedicated Punjabi apps. Ladoo Pro ($9.99 a month or $69.99 a year, 7-day trial) removes the daily limits on tutor chat and word cards and opens all the stories.

Where it is not the pick: Ladoo teaches Gurmukhi, not Shahmukhi, so learners focused on written Punjabi from Pakistan will want a tutor alongside it. And if you want one subscription covering many languages, a multi-language app fits better.

2. Ling: best gamified exercise volume

Ling covers 60+ languages with a Duolingo-style loop of taps, matches, and streaks, and its Punjabi course has the largest exercise volume of the gamified apps. It teaches Gurmukhi within the lessons and includes chatbot dialogues. The tradeoff is that one template serves all 60+ languages, so Punjabi gets the same exercise shapes as everything else, and the cultural context is thin. A solid choice if the Duolingo feel is what keeps you showing up.

3. Pimsleur Punjabi: best audio-only option

Pimsleur's 30-minute audio lessons are built on prompted recall: you hear a phrase, produce it out loud, and the schedule brings it back right before you forget. It is the strongest option for commutes and produces good pronunciation habits. It does not teach the Gurmukhi script at all, so pair it with something that does if reading matters to you.

4. Mango Languages: best if your library has it

Mango teaches through short recorded dialogues with native-speaker audio and clear grammar notes. Its best feature is the price for many people: hundreds of public library systems in the US and Canada offer Mango free with a library card. Check your library before paying for anything.

5. PunjabiCharm: best for young kids

PunjabiCharm is built around human voice recordings and a bright, game-like interface that lands well with children, and it has a large, established user base. For adults who want grammar, speaking feedback, or conversation practice it runs out of depth sooner than the apps above.

6. Preply and italki: fastest route to conversation

A human tutor is still the fastest way to real conversation, and marketplaces like Preply and italki have Punjabi tutors from a few dollars an hour. The catch is scheduling and cost per hour compared to an app. The strongest setup we know is an app for daily script and vocabulary work plus a weekly tutor session to use it out loud.

How to actually choose

If you grew up hearing Punjabi and want to finally speak it, or you want to read Gurmukhi, start with Ladoo; the whole curriculum is free, so trying it costs nothing. If you want maximum gamified volume, take Ling. Audio in the car, Pimsleur. Free through your library, Mango. A young kid, PunjabiCharm. And whichever you pick, a weekly hour with a human tutor multiplies all of them.

Common questions

What is the best app to learn Punjabi?

For most learners, and especially heritage speakers, we think it is Ladoo: it is the most complete app for speaking Punjabi and reading Gurmukhi, every lesson is free, and it is the only app on this list with an AI tutor you can speak Punjabi with. If you want maximum gamified exercise volume, Ling is the strongest alternative. If you only want audio for the car, pick Pimsleur.

What is the best free app to learn Punjabi?

Ladoo has the largest free tier of the dedicated Punjabi apps: all 170+ lessons in both tracks are free, with no lesson ever locked. Mango Languages is completely free if your public library subscribes to it, which many in the US and Canada do.

Which Punjabi apps teach the Gurmukhi script?

Ladoo has a dedicated Gurmukhi track that teaches all 35 letters, vowel signs, and numerals from zero, with audio and finger tracing. Ling teaches Gurmukhi within its lesson flow. Pimsleur is audio only and does not teach the script, and tutor marketplaces depend on the tutor you pick.

Can I learn Punjabi on Duolingo?

No. Duolingo has no Punjabi course and its community course incubator closed in 2021, so none is in development either.
