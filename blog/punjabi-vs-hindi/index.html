Punjabi vs Hindi: how different are they?

By the Ladoo team · Published October 3, 2026 · Updated October 3, 2026

The grammar is close to identical. The small words are different, the scripts are different, and Punjabi has tones while Hindi has none. A Hindi speaker can follow a slow Punjabi conversation and still sound like a Hindi speaker the moment they open their mouth.

Punjabi is its own language

Punjabi is not a dialect of Hindi. Both come from the same Indo-Aryan branch, the way Spanish and Italian both come from Latin. Punjabi has its own written literature going back to Baba Farid, born in 1173, whose verses are in the Guru Granth Sahib, and it is one of the 22 languages listed in the Eighth Schedule of India's constitution.

What is the same

The verb goes at the end of the sentence in both. Both have two genders, and verbs change with the gender of the speaker: ਮੈਂ ਜਾਂਦਾ ਹਾਂ (main janda haan) if a man says "I go," ਮੈਂ ਜਾਂਦੀ ਹਾਂ (main jandi haan) if a woman does. Hindi works the same way. Both use ne in the past tense, both put "in" and "from" after the noun instead of before it, and a lot of everyday nouns are shared outright: ghar, paani, din, raat, kitaab.

The small words are where they split

Most of the difference sits in the words you use twenty times a minute. Here they are side by side, Punjabi first.

  • what: ਕੀ ki / क्या kya
  • from: ਤੋਂ ton / से se
  • to (him, her, me): ਨੂੰ nu / को ko
  • of: ਦਾ da / का ka
  • in: ਵਿੱਚ vich / में mein
  • with: ਨਾਲ naal / के साथ ke saath
  • now: ਹੁਣ hun / अब ab
  • where: ਕਿੱਥੇ kithe / कहाँ kahan
  • how: ਕਿਵੇਂ kiven / कैसे kaise
  • here: ਇੱਥੇ ithe / यहाँ yahan
  • we: ਅਸੀਂ asin / हम hum
  • you (polite): ਤੁਸੀਂ tusi / आप aap
  • to me: ਮੈਨੂੰ mainu / मुझे mujhe
  • I will go: ਮੈਂ ਜਾਵਾਂਗਾ main javanga / मैं जाऊँगा main jaunga

Put them together and the same sentence comes out looking related but clearly separate. "Where are you coming from?" is ਤੁਸੀਂ ਕਿੱਥੋਂ ਆ ਰਹੇ ਹੋ? (tusi kithon aa rahe ho?) in Punjabi and आप कहाँ से आ रहे हैं? (aap kahan se aa rahe hain?) in Hindi. Same order, same tense, almost no shared words.

There is also a sound pattern worth knowing. Where Hindi has a long vowel and a single consonant, Punjabi often has a short vowel and a doubled one: Hindi kaam, Punjabi kamm (work). Hindi haath, Punjabi hatth (hand). Hindi aaj, Punjabi ajj (today). Hindi saat, Punjabi satt (seven). Once you hear it you cannot stop hearing it.

Punjabi has tones

This is the biggest difference, and the one Hindi speakers miss. Punjabi has three tones. Hindi has none. The word for house is spelled ghar in both languages, but a Hindi speaker says it with a breathy gh, and a Punjabi speaker says something closer to "kar" with the pitch dipping low. Letters like ਘ, ਝ, ਢ, ਧ, and ਭ lost their breathiness in Punjabi and left a tone behind. Our pronunciation guide has the pairs with audio.

Different scripts

Hindi is written in Devanagari. Punjabi is written in Gurmukhi or Shahmukhi. Gurmukhi and Devanagari come from the same ancestor script, so a few letters look like cousins, but you cannot read one with the other. Gurmukhi has 35 core letters and spells words the way they sound, so most people can read simple words within a few weeks (the full chart is here). Shahmukhi uses the same alphabet as Urdu, so anyone who reads Urdu has most of it already (more on that here).

Can Hindi and Punjabi speakers understand each other?

Partly, and it is lopsided. Most Punjabi speakers understand Hindi very well, because they grew up on Hindi films and TV. Hindi speakers tend to follow a good amount of Punjabi, a lot of it learned from Punjabi songs, and then lose the thread when an auntie speeds up.

If you already speak Hindi

You have the grammar done. What is left is the list above, the tones, and a script. The usual mistake is putting Hindi words into Punjabi sentences. "Main ghar se aaya" is Hindi said with a Punjabi accent. In Punjabi it is ਮੈਂ ਘਰੋਂ ਆਇਆ (main gharon aaya). Fix the small words first and you will sound Punjabi much faster than you expect.
