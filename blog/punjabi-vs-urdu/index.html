Punjabi vs Urdu: how different are they?

By the Ladoo team · Published November 10, 2026 · Updated November 10, 2026

Punjabi and Urdu are two languages. The grammar is nearly the same, and when Punjabi is written in Shahmukhi the two share an alphabet. The small words are different, and Punjabi has tones while Urdu has none. An Urdu reader can sound out a line of Punjabi and still not know what half of it means.

Same letters, different language

Shahmukhi is Punjabi written in the Perso-Arabic script. It uses the letters Urdu uses, in the same Nastaliq hand, so a page of Punjabi and a page of Urdu look alike until you read them. "What is your name?" is تہاڈا نام کی اے؟ (tuhada naam ki ae?) in Punjabi and آپ کا نام کیا ہے؟ (aap ka naam kya hai?) in Urdu. Only naam is the same.

Punjabi is also written in Gurmukhi, which runs left to right and looks nothing like Urdu: ਤੁਹਾਡਾ ਨਾਮ ਕੀ ਹੈ? That is the same Punjabi sentence. The two scripts are side by side in Gurmukhi vs Shahmukhi.

What is the same

The verb goes at the end of the sentence in both. Both have two genders, and the verb changes with the gender of the speaker. Both put "in" and "from" after the noun, and both use ne in the past tense.

A lot of nouns are shared too. Punjabi and Urdu both took in Persian and Arabic words, so kitaab (book), dost (friend), dil (heart), and khush (happy) are everyday words in both. People who write Punjabi in Shahmukhi tend to say shukriya for thanks, the Urdu word. People who write it in Gurmukhi tend to say dhannvad.

The small words are where they split

Most of the difference is in the words you say twenty times a minute. Punjabi first, then Urdu.

  • what: کی ki / کیا kya
  • to (him, her, me): نوں nu / کو ko
  • of: دا da / کا ka
  • in: وچ vich / میں mein
  • from: توں ton / سے se
  • with: نال naal / کے ساتھ ke saath
  • now: ہن hun / اب ab
  • where: کتھے kithe / کہاں kahan
  • here: ایتھے ithe / یہاں yahan
  • we: اسی asin / ہم hum
  • you (polite): تسی tusi / آپ aap
  • your (polite): تہاڈا tuhada / آپ کا aap ka
  • to me: مینوں mainu / مجھے mujhe

"Where are you?" is تسی کتھے ہو؟ (tusi kithe ho?) in Punjabi and آپ کہاں ہیں؟ (aap kahan hain?) in Urdu. The order is the same and the tense is the same. None of the words are.

The sounds shift in a regular way too. Where Urdu has a long vowel and one consonant, Punjabi often has a short vowel and a doubled consonant. Urdu kaam, Punjabi kamm (work). Urdu haath, Punjabi hatth (hand). Urdu aaj, Punjabi ajj (today). Urdu saat, Punjabi satt (seven). You can see it on the page: work is کام in Urdu and کم in Punjabi.

Punjabi has tones

Urdu has no tones. Punjabi has three. The word for house is written گھر in both languages. An Urdu speaker says ghar with a breathy gh. A Punjabi speaker says something closer to "kar" with the pitch dipping low. Shahmukhi does not mark the tone, so the spelling gives you no warning, and this is how you can tell an Urdu speaker reading Punjabi aloud. Our pronunciation guide has the tone pairs with audio.

Can Urdu and Punjabi speakers understand each other?

Partly, and it is lopsided. In Pakistan's 2023 census, 37% of people named Punjabi as their mother tongue and 9% named Urdu. Urdu is the language of school, news, and government there, so most Punjabi speakers in the country also speak Urdu. It does not work as well in the other direction. An Urdu speaker who did not grow up around Punjabi follows the gist of a slow conversation and loses it when the small words pile up.

Did Urdu come from Punjabi?

One scholar argued that it did. In Punjab Mein Urdu (1928), Hafiz Mahmood Sherani wrote that Urdu took shape around Lahore, out of an early form of Punjabi, and moved to Delhi afterwards. Masud Husain Khan and other linguists disagreed and put its roots in the speech around Delhi. The argument has never been settled. Either way, the two languages grew up next to each other, and that is why so much of the grammar matches.

If you already speak Urdu

You have the grammar, and if you read Urdu you have the alphabet. What is left is the list above and the tones. The usual mistake is putting Urdu words into a Punjabi sentence. "Mujhe paani do" in a Punjabi accent is still Urdu. In Punjabi it is مینوں پانی دیو (mainu paani deo).

Common questions

Are Punjabi and Urdu the same language?

No. Punjabi and Urdu are separate languages with similar grammar and many shared nouns. The everyday small words are different, and Punjabi has tones while Urdu does not.

Is Shahmukhi the same as Urdu?

No. Urdu is a language. Shahmukhi is the Perso-Arabic script that Punjabi is written in. The letters are the same, so an Urdu reader can sound out Shahmukhi, but the words are Punjabi.

Can Urdu speakers understand Punjabi?

Partly. An Urdu speaker follows the gist of slow Punjabi because the grammar and many nouns match. Most Punjabi speakers who went to school in Urdu understand it fully, so it is easier in that direction.

Is Punjabi closer to Hindi or to Urdu?

About the same. Spoken Hindi and Urdu are very close to each other, so Punjabi is as far from one as from the other. On paper it depends on the script: Punjabi in Shahmukhi looks like Urdu, and Punjabi in Gurmukhi looks like neither.

Is Punjabi hard to learn if you speak Urdu?

It is one of the easier languages for an Urdu speaker to learn. The sentence structure carries over, and so does the alphabet if you learn Punjabi in Shahmukhi. The work is in the small words and the three tones.
