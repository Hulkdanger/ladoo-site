Punjabi greetings: hello, thank you, and goodbye, with audio

By the Ladoo team · Published August 21, 2026

The Punjabi hello is ਸਤਿ ਸ੍ਰੀ ਅਕਾਲ (sat sri akal), and unlike English greetings it works at any hour, with anyone, in any mood. Every word on this page has a play button with the same audio used in the Ladoo app, because Punjabi is tonal and reading a romanization only gets you halfway.

The essentials

Tap play, listen, then say it out loud. Reading silently will not put the words in your mouth.

ਸਤਿ ਸ੍ਰੀ ਅਕਾਲ sat srī akāl hello (the Sikh greeting)

The universal Punjabi greeting, literally 'God is the eternal truth'. Works at any time of day, with anyone.

ਹੈਲੋ hailo hello (on the phone)

Borrowed from English and used constantly, mostly when answering the phone.

ਹਾਂ ਜੀ hāṃ jī yes (polite)

The ji makes it respectful. Safe default with elders and strangers.

ਨਾ ਜੀ nā jī no (polite)

A soft, respectful no.

ਕਿਰਪਾ ਕਰਕੇ kirpā karke please

Formal please. Everyday Punjabi often softens requests with ji instead.

ਧੰਨਵਾਦ dhannvād thank you

The standard thank you.

ਸ਼ੁਕਰੀਆ shukrīā thanks

A warmer, everyday thanks, shared with Urdu.

ਰੱਬ ਰਾਖਾ rabb rākhā goodbye (God protect you)

The traditional farewell.

ਮਿਲਾਂਗੇ milānge we will meet (again)

How you say 'see you later': pher milange, we'll meet again.

ਸੁਆਗਤ suāgat welcome

As in welcoming a guest: ji aayan nu is the fuller phrase you'll hear at doors.

ਠੀਕ ṭhīk fine / okay

The answer to 'how are you': main thik haan, I am fine.

ਹਾਲ hāl condition (how one is)

The word inside 'how are you': ki haal hai? What's your condition?

How to ask "how are you?"

The everyday question is ਕੀ ਹਾਲ ਹੈ? (ki haal hai?), literally "what is your condition?". Warmer, to someone familiar: ki haal chaal hai? The standard reply is main thik haan, "I am fine", usually followed by asking right back. With elders, add ji to almost anything and you cannot go far wrong.

A note on sat sri akal

Sat sri akal is the Sikh greeting, literally "God is the eternal truth", and it doubles as both hello and goodbye, the way "shalom" or "salaam" does. Muslim Punjabi speakers greet with as-salaam alaikum, and you will also hear namaste from Hindu Punjabi speakers. In the diaspora, sat sri akal is the safe, warm default almost everywhere.
