Punjabi food words: chaa, roti, and everything on the table

By the Ladoo team · Published August 21, 2026

Nobody gets out of a Punjabi home without being fed, so the kitchen is where a learner's vocabulary earns its keep fastest. Start with ਚਾਹ (chaa, tea) and ਰੋਟੀ (roti), and know that "roti kha lai?", literally "have you eaten roti?", is really asking "have you eaten at all?", because roti stands in for the whole meal.

The words

ਚਾਹ chāh tea (chaa)

Not 'chai tea'. Just chaa, and it is offered within minutes of you sitting down.

ਪਾਣੀ pāṇī water
ਦੁੱਧ duddh milk
ਰੋਟੀ roṭī roti / bread

Also means the meal itself: roti kha lai? means 'have you eaten?'

ਸਬਜ਼ੀ sabzī vegetable dish
ਦਹੀਂ dahīṃ yogurt
ਚੌਲ chaul rice
ਲੂਣ lūṇ salt
ਮਿੱਠਾ miṭṭhā sweet (taste)
ਮਿਠਾਈ miṭhāī sweets

The box from the sweet shop. A ladoo is the round one this app is named after.

ਬਰਫ਼ੀ barfī barfi (milk sweet)
ਖੀਰ khīr kheer (rice pudding)
ਨਮਕੀਨ namkīn salty
ਖਾਓ khāo eat (polite command)

What every Punjabi grandmother says on loop.

ਭੁੱਖ bhukkh hunger

bhukkh laggi hai: I'm hungry.

The two commands you will hear forever

Khao, "eat", and its gentler cousin hor lao, "take more", are the soundtrack of every Punjabi kitchen. Declining is a ritual with rounds: the first na ji is never taken seriously, the second is negotiated, and by the third you have usually already been served. Learning to lose this game gracefully is part of learning Punjabi.
